Are Poker Solvers Allowed? Where the Line Is, Online and Live

By Rohit Kaul · August 30, 2026 · 15 min read

If you study with a solver, a trainer, or a tracker, you've probably had the thought at least once: is this actually allowed? The short answer is yes. The long answer is a stack of rules that all turn on one word: when. Own the tool, run the tool, review with the tool. Just don't have it open with a hand in progress.

The timing makes it worth spelling out now. WSOP Online 2026 is running on GGPoker as we write this, from August 16 to September 29, on the network that suspended 31 accounts with GTO Wizard's help. This June, the WSOP named GTO Wizard, the same tool, its official training partner and started showing its Optimal Play graphic on the broadcast. Solver on the screen, solver banned in the room. This post lays out the rules online and live, the cases that decided what counts as during play, and what it means for a phone with a study app on it.

TL;DR: Solvers, trainers, and trackers are allowed — away from the table. PokerStars bans them only while its client is running. GGPoker bans any outside help during play, full stop. GTO Wizard's own terms tell you not to use it during a game. Live, the 2024 TDA rules keep strategy tools off the table, and the 2026 WSOP cash-game rules now list solver output by name. Intent doesn't save you: a UK pro lost a £40,000 sponsorship in February 2026 for checking hands after they were played, with the client still open. TableLab is built for the allowed side of that line, which is post-session review. Here's where the line sits.

Are poker solvers allowed?

Yes, as study tools. No operator bans owning or running a solver; every major one bans using it while you're in a hand. PokerStars puts solvers in the bucket that's off-limits only while its software is running, and GTO Wizard's terms tell you not to use the service during a game, online included. The line is when, not what.

Look at how PokerStars structures its policy, because the structure is the answer. The page sorts everything into three buckets: tools that are always fine, tools that are never fine, and tools that are fine except while the PokerStars client is running (PokerStars). Solvers, range-versus-range simulators, ICM and Nash calculators all land in that third bucket. Not the second. The tool is permitted; the timing is the offense. The exact wording of that third heading is worth memorizing, because it decides the Clack case later on: "prohibited while our software is running."

The vendor's own contract says the same thing. Section 7.1 of GTO Wizard's terms bars using the service during a live poker game, and spells out that online games count and that this is what real-time assistance means (GTO Wizard Terms). Nobody selling solvers wants to be the reason a customer gets banned, so the clause sits in the contract you clicked through.

What's actually banned, then, is real-time assistance, or RTA. PokerNews' working definition from 2020 still holds: anything that helps you make a decision while a cash game or tournament is in progress (PokerNews). There's no list of software in that definition, only a clock.

What counts as real-time assistance?

Anything that influences a decision while a hand is live: solver output, range or ICM calculators, push/fold charts, a friend on Discord. GGPoker's current policy goes furthest and bans looking at charts of any kind during play. PokerStars still allows a simple starting-hand chart for unopened pots, which is the one place the two disagree.

It helps to sort assistance into three families. Software: solvers, equity and ICM calculators, bots. Reference material: preflop charts, push/fold tables, a printed range. Human help: a coach on the rail, a friend on a call. GGPoker's policy (version 20260313) covers all three with one sentence in §2.2: "Every decision made at the poker table should be made free of any external assistance" (GGPoker). Its list of blocked tools then names the usual suspects, RTA, bots, solvers, charts and HUDs, as examples of what influences a decision.

The chart split is the detail that catches people. PokerStars permits a simple table of which hands to open in an unopened pot (PokerStars). GGPoker's §2.3 bans charts of any kind during play. Same laminated RFI chart, two verdicts. If you play on both networks, follow the stricter rule and leave it in the drawer.

HUDs are their own category, and the policies split again. PokerStars allows a HUD only if every number in it came from hands you played yourself, and it bans datamining outright. GGPoker blocks third-party HUDs altogether. Live, the question never comes up: there's no HUD, and you build reads by hand, which is the skill we covered in reading players without a HUD.

Our read: the operators aren't banning math. They're banning outsourced math. If the number came from your head, it's poker. If it came from a screen during the hand, it's RTA.

The big sites, in their own words

Same principle, different wording. PokerStars sorts tools into permitted, prohibited at all times, and permitted-but-not-while-the-client-runs. GGPoker bans RTA, bots, solvers, charts, and third-party HUDs outright, and §2.4 sets the penalty as a permanent ban with funds confiscated. HUD policy is where sites diverge most.

Two GGPoker clauses matter more than the rest. The penalty line, §2.4, is written in capitals: "PERMANENT BAN, CONFISCATION OF FUNDS." It's the only penalty the RTA section names. The safe-harbor line is quieter: a tool that doesn't interact with GGPoker during gameplay won't be flagged. That's the clause a post-session tracker or an offline solver lives under, and it's worth knowing by name.

On HUDs, the industry splits down the middle. GipsyTeam's May 2026 survey lists PokerStars, 888, WPN, iPoker, and Winamax as permitting HUDs, and GGPoker, WPT Global, and CoinPoker as prohibiting them (GipsyTeam). It's a useful map, but the operator's page is the territory, and operators edit those pages without announcing it.

What are the rules at a live table?

Stricter than online, and written down. TDA Rule 5D (2024) keeps betting apps, charts, and any other strategy tool off the table. WSOP 2026 Rule 64(g) bars any poker information tool while you're in a hand, and 64(d) extends the ban to spectators in the room. Even the WSOP cash-game rules now list solver output by name, in Rule 21.

Tournament rules: TDA 5 and WSOP 63–64

Start with the Poker TDA, whose rules many card rooms adopt. The 2024 rules (version 1.0, dated October 9, 2024) rewrote Rule 5 into a device section with three teeth (Poker TDA). 5B: phones and other devices can't rest on the table. 5C: if you have a live hand, you can't touch a device at all. 5D is the one that matters here, so here it is in full: "Betting apps, charts, and other poker strategy tools may not be used at the table. Nor may players receive or use poker strategy data from another person or source." Notice there's no software test in it. A paper chart fails it too.

The WSOP goes further in its 2026 tournament rules (WSOP 2026 Tournament Rules). Rule 63: no phone or other communication device on the table, ever. Rule 64(b) allows approved devices only if they contain no AI and no other form of electronic assistance. 64(c) removes devices entirely once the field is down to three tables. 64(d) widens the net to the rail: nobody in the tournament room, players or spectators, may use charts, apps, AI, or any other electronic assistance.

Then 64(g), the clause aimed at you: no betting apps, gaming charts, or any poker information tool while you're involved in a hand. The carve-out is narrow and named: the WSOP LIVE app, WSOP.com, and the Caesars Mobile Sports App. Rule 64(a) quotes Nevada's device statute, NRS 465.075, so the rulebook isn't only house policy.

Cash games: WSOP Live-Action Rule 21

Cash games are covered too. The WSOP's 2026 Live-Action Rules, Rule 21, prohibit real-time assistance outright. Players can't use any device, software, or communication method to get "coaching, advice, analysis, solver output, or other strategic assistance about any live hand in play," and the rule names smart watches and earpieces so nobody can claim a grey area (WSOP 2026 Live-Action Rules). Penalties run from immediate removal through forfeiture of chips to a permanent ban pending investigation. Rule 27 allows devices that don't interfere with the game, and Rule 82 calls phone use at the table discouraged. That word is doing a lot of work.

Why 2026 is the year the line got enforced

Detection moved from noticing suspicious play to timestamping it. GTO Wizard's Fair Play Check returns the exact time a board was solved; GGPoker used it to suspend 31 accounts on the first day of their partnership, announced March 2025. In 2026 an iPoker suspension cost a UK pro a £40,000 deal, and the WSOP put GTO Wizard on its broadcast while banning it in the room.

2020: pattern detection

The enforcement story starts in September 2020, when GGPoker answered a cheating scandal with numbers instead of a statement. Forty accounts banned, forty warned, $1,175,305 confiscated from 13 accounts, and 4,329 players reimbursed, about $272 each (PokerNews; pokerfuse). GGPoker didn't say how it found them.

2023–2026: timestamps

September 2023 changed the method. GTO Wizard's Fair Play Check lets an operator ask whether a given board, or any strategically identical board, was solved in GTO Wizard inside a date window, and if it was, the tool returns the exact time (GTO Wizard). WPN and WPT Global signed on the same month (poker.org). Weeks later PokerStars said it catches 95% of RTA proactively and called RTA an existential threat to online poker (pokerfuse). That's the operator's claim, not an audited figure.

In March 2025 GGPoker joined, citing suspiciously heavy GTO Wizard use during live play and summing up the principle in one line: "Poker should be individual vs. individual, not individual vs. machine" (GGPoker; PokerNews). GTO Wizard later reported 31 accounts suspended on the first day of the cooperation, and said operators can now check at scale whether boards dealt on their network were being looked up in real time (GTO Wizard). ACR formalized its own integration in June 2025 (ACR).

Which brings us to 2026. In February, an iPoker suspension cost Thomas Clack a £40,000 Grosvenor package; the next section is about him. In June, the WSOP named GTO Wizard its official training partner, and the broadcast now carries an Optimal Play graphic showing the solver's preferred action and whether the player followed it (PokerNews; GTO Wizard). And from August 16 to September 29, WSOP Online 2026 runs on GGPoker: 33 bracelets and a $25 million guaranteed Main Event (PokerNews). That's the network that suspends you for opening the broadcast's sponsor mid-hand. That isn't hypocrisy. It's the line, drawn in public.

Does "I only checked it after the hand" count?

On PokerStars' wording, yes, and iPoker's February 2026 suspension of Thomas Clack is the test case. Thomas Clack said he only looked hands up after they'd been played. It still cost him a £40,000 Grosvenor sponsorship. The rule is about the tool being open while the client runs, not about intent. ACR is the counter-example: it investigated Nacho Barbero and cleared him.

Clack's full quote, as PokerNews reported it in February 2026: "After a hand had been played, I looked it up to see if I played it well. I wasn't using it to affect my play, but I had it open, which was silly" (PokerNews). Grosvenor ended the sponsorship anyway. He's still allowed to play live at Grosvenor venues, which tells you the operator separated the person from the policy.

Re-read PokerStars' third category with that in mind: prohibited while our software is running. Not while you're in a hand. Not when it affects a decision. Running. On a plain reading, reviewing a hand you've already folded, with the client minimized behind the solver, satisfies every word of that clause.

The contrast case is Nacho Barbero. In January 2025 he was seen with GTO Wizard open during The Venom on ACR, which he said was for coaching on Discord (PokerNews). ACR investigated hand by hand and cleared him, saying he never used real-time assistance to help his own or anyone else's decisions during live hands (PokerNews). He kept his seat. He also spent weeks under a public investigation over a window that was open.

One more thing to know about the vendor. Section 7.2 of GTO Wizard's terms lets the company confirm to a third party, anonymously, whether a particular board was searched in the service at a particular time (GTO Wizard Terms). The solver you pay for is a witness for the operator, not an ally in a dispute. So the practical rule writes itself: close the client, then study.

Where TableLab sits: the after-the-session side

On the allowed side, by design. TableLab has no online-client integration, no HUD, and no hand-history scraping; the hand recorder, the GTO library, and the AI review are post-session tools. At a live table the only thing you'd touch mid-session is the session logger, and TDA 5B and 5C make that a between-hands, phone-off-the-table activity, so treat it that way.

Where we sit: TableLab is a bankroll tracker with a hand recorder, a browsable GTO library, and AI hand review. The GTO numbers come from a real CFR solver (TexasSolver) we run offline. The AI only explains those numbers and reasons about spots a solver can't cover. It's barred from stating a frequency it wasn't given: a language model is not a solver. Nothing in the app connects to an online client, reads a table, or shows opponent stats, and every review happens after the session. That's the allowed side of every policy quoted above. Two honest caveats. The analysis can be wrong, so verify big decisions. And at a live table your phone answers to the house rule, not ours.

Two practical notes on the study side. The GTO numbers you browse in the app come from a solved GTO library of 26,325 spots, computed offline and served as static data. And because the review runs after the session, it can sit next to your tracked win rate: the hand you're second-guessing and the sample it belongs to, in one place.

The live table is where a study app needs manners. Log rebuys, breaks, and expenses with your hand folded, never with a live hand (5C). Keep the phone in your lap or pocket, never resting on the felt (5B). Don't open the hand recorder, the library, or a past review while you're in a hand; that's 5D territory, whatever the app is. And at the WSOP, once you reach the final three tables, nothing at all, per 64(c). In our own sessions the logger comes out with the cards in the muck, and the phone goes back in the pocket before the next deal. The house rule always outranks the app's design.

How do you study with solvers without crossing the line?

Four habits keep you clean everywhere, online and live. If a rule is ambiguous, the floor decides, and not meaning to hasn't saved anyone yet.

  • Client closed, then solver open. Not minimized. Closed.
  • Charts stay off the desk and off the table. PokerStars permits a simple preflop chart in unopened pots; GGPoker permits none; TDA 5D permits none. The strictest rule is the one to memorize.
  • Review after the session, not between hands. Clack's account is exactly what between hands costs, and PokerStars' rule is about the software running, so a window behind a window still counts. Record the hand, finish the session, then open the review.
  • Live, assume TDA Rule 5 unless the room posts otherwise.

If you suspect someone else, there's a tool for that too. GTO Wizard's Fair Play Check is open to players as well as operators, and it reports whether a board was searched inside a date window, with built-in delays (GTO Wizard). Use it before you post the accusation.

And keep the long view. Most of the moments where you're sure someone is on a solver are variance wearing a costume. Run your numbers through our variance calculator before you decide a bad month was anything but a bad month.

FAQ

Are poker solvers allowed?

Yes, for study. PokerStars bans solvers only while its client is running (PokerStars); GGPoker bans any outside help during play; GTO Wizard's own terms bar use during a game. Own one, run one, review with one. Just not with a hand in progress.

Is using a solver cheating?

During a hand, yes. That's real-time assistance, and every major site treats it as cheating; GGPoker's stated penalty is a permanent ban with funds confiscated (GGPoker). After the session, with the client closed, it's how everyone serious studies, and no operator objects to it.

Can you check a hand in a solver between hands or on a break?

Don't. Thomas Clack said he only looked hands up after they'd been played and still lost a £40,000 Grosvenor sponsorship after an iPoker suspension in February 2026 (PokerNews). The rule keys on the tool being open while the client runs, not on your intent.

Are HUDs allowed in 2026?

It depends on the site. PokerStars permits HUDs built only from hands you played yourself and bans datamining; GGPoker and WPT Global prohibit third-party HUDs (GipsyTeam). Live, there's no HUD at all, so you build reads by hand and write them down.

Can you use your phone at a live poker table?

Under the 2024 TDA rules: not with a live hand (Rule 5C), never resting on the table (5B), and never for strategy tools (5D) (Poker TDA). The 2026 WSOP goes further: devices removed at the final three tables, and charts, apps, and artificial intelligence banned in the tournament room for players and spectators alike.

Is a bankroll tracker app allowed?

Yes. A tracker doesn't touch the hand in progress. Online, PokerStars permits tools that use only data from your own play, and GGPoker doesn't flag tools that don't interact with its client during gameplay. Live, log between hands with the phone off the felt.

The bottom line

Solvers compute; you decide. That's the whole principle, and every rule-writer quoted here draws it in the same place: the line is when, not what. Intent isn't a defense, a minimized window is still a running one, and the vendor's terms let it confirm the timestamp if asked. Study hard, with the client closed. Live, keep the phone off the felt and out of your hand while you've got cards. TableLab lives on the after-the-session side of that line on purpose. Record the hand, log the session, and open the review once the cards are in the muck.
